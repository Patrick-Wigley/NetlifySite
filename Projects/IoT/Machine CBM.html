Overview

As maintenance strategies and downtime reduction remain as a prevalent topic for manufacturing industries, Industry 4.0 (I4.0) solutions are becoming more widely considerable, such as Conditional-Based Monitoring (CBM). Although this endeavour can be a novelty for industries, and the complexities of at each milestone can be puzzling, as there is not a strong standardised approach for the implementation of an entire CBM solution. Therefore, this study presents a journey, documenting, testing, analysing and discussing the outcomes of our approaches at each milestone, to help contribute to the standardisation of implementing entire CBM solutions, from the ground upwards.

Motivation

The uncertain lifespan of machine components and their spontaneous failures can bring processes to a halt. Consequently, downtime of functioning machines impacts the productive and efficient workflows in manufacturing environments, especially when forced to wait for the arrival of replacements. A recent survey conducted by Fluke UK Ltd reveals that 68% of UK manufacturers suffered downtime in the past year with 46% reporting 6-10 downtime incidents a week; At an average loss of £1.36M per hour. (Fluke UK Ltd, 2025). This issue is global, with other surveys discovering astronomical, consequential economic losses in revenue & expenses, due to unplanned downtime & inefficient maintenance strategies (Thomas & Weiss, 2020)

Although, with the rise of I4.0 technologies, such as Internet of Things (IoT), Machine Learning (ML) and Digital Twins (DT), an industries assets and their behavouirs can become much more verbose to maintenance engineers. A connected microcontroller with a sensor on a machine (IoT) can pass live data to a ML component, for pattern/trend analysis and anomaly discovery, giving insights into a machines state and the gradual underlying behavouiral changes. This can be shown comprehensively in a dashboard or, within an immersive Digital Twin (DT) environment, where an industries manufacturing environment can be traversed digitally, and their machines can be interacted with, showing conditional effects such as local colour changes on the asset to indicate health (100% is green, 0% is red) or asset-status labelling to describe component failures.

This is a known phenomena now, known as Conditional Based Monitoring. Industries are opting for these technologies although, where to start? how easy is it? and is this possible for an industry to implement this themselves, with cheaper components? My study aimed to investigate this, with a self-built CBM solution prototype, consisting of the foundations which can manifest into ambitious applications! During this study I took some "unauthodox" approaches, such as 3D printing the IoT components enclosure, so read on if you're interested and see where I am at currently with this

Key Idea: Develop a self-built CBM solution, providing a means of anticipation, with early-stage discovery and developmental degradation, giving justitifcation for the scheduling of the next maintenance timing and the requiring attention from the machine being monitored.

The Prototype

I have not gone into great details here, please review my paper for full technicality. Although, I wanted to demonstrate some intesting aspects in this CBM solution.

Data Acquisition

In this CBM solution, vibrations are observed through accelerations measurements. Therefore, the MMA8451 Adafruit accelerometer is used with an Esp32 microcontroller. This is capable of capturing samples upto 800Hz frequency; Each acceleration signal captured is a second long, thus yielding 800 samples. Signals are captured periodically (10 minute interval).

Figure 1. IIoT component



Great, this PCB board hosting the accelerometer can just be appended to a machine right! Well, it isn't exactly as straight forwards as this, nor appropriate. There are a multitude of factors that need considering, including external factors (environment, users, work-loads). Therefore, unconventially I designed and 3D printed an enclosure for the electronics used. Typically, industrial sensors would come with a strong metalic enclosure, ensuring a high ingress rating (IP64+), operating temperature range, and etc.

Figure 2. Enclosure Design & Print





The Esp is programmed using ArduinoIDE, effectively establishing Wi-Fi & serial connectivity then, capturing the signal, performing some preprocessing & transmitting to MathWorks Thingspeak API.

The ML component does not use the 800 raw samples, but the features. And as ThingSpeak channels can only hold 8 single-valued fields, the feature extraction is performed on edge. These features are then sent to the API, condensing the raw datasamples into uniquely meaningful statistical scalars. Feature engineering has been proving its worth within ML, and for improving accuracy in contrary to just using the raw datasamples. Features extracted are within time & frequency domain, these are:
  • Standard Devation
  • Mean
  • Kurtosis
  • Skewness
  • Peak-to-Peak
  • Root Mean Square
  • Crest Factor
  • Spectral Spread
  • Spectral Centroid
  • Spectral Total
  • Spectral Entropy
  • Spectral Peak

Convienently, there mathematical definitions can be found in literature, these was referenced and implemented within the Esps code.

Figure 3 & 4. Feature engineering & IoT Cloud Storage

Anomaly Discovery & Health Estimation

The livestreaming data from the sensor node(s) are pulled from the API into a python script, to be preprocessed (cleaned, standardised and rolling averaged for reliablity, accuracy and fair assessment). The ML component uses an autoencoder, which is a Recurrent Nureal Network (RNN) based decoding & encoding architecture. Reconstructuring the learned healthy patterns at the current time. The accuracy of these reconstructions are the fundemental component needed to determine the machines behavioural changes. This is known as the reconstruction errors, calcuated using Mean Squared Error (MSE), assessing the distance between this predicted reconstructed features against the actual feature values at this corresponding datapoint.
These MSEs are then smoothed, using an exponentially weighted moving averages, which gives more impact to the most recent datapoints. This allowes older anomalies to have less impact on overall score, as they could have been natural hiccups, and more recent trends are most concerning, as these could be sudden breakages which need attention. If anomalies continue to arise, these accumalatively impact the health score. If anomalies die down, the health score slowly rises back up, but neither back to the normal baseline, retaining memory of previous abnormal events. Finally, the anomaly scores are negated & used as exponents to eulers constant number, which effectively keeps the score constrained to the range of 1.0-0. Try it! See 2.7 ** -0 = 1, 2.7 ** -0.3 = 0.74, 2.7 ** -0.6 = 0.55 and so on! (negative exponents always yield x < 1)

Digital Twin

The ML script is integrated into an Unreal Engine 5 environment, computing the machines health in live time. The industries machine assets can be portrayed and interacted with in the digital environment, respresented as synommonous meshes (my example uses the UE5 default model mesh due to limitations of the study). Finally, the health-score is applied as a colour mask the models mesh. Effectively making the machines digital replicas colour redder the lower the health-score is.
